Since I was a kid I've had cold feet. It's been very bothersome in winter but gladly, in my hometown, the wheather gets cold only at January and February. To be more specific, by cold I mean nothing like many other western countries, I'm now checking with Gemini and is about 17º/8º of maximum and minimum in Januray. My cold feet only become a problem if I remain sit for several hours. At first is uncomfortable, then becomes very unsettling and, in the end, if I don't take measures I usually get a cold.

Through the years, I've tried wearing two pairs of socks, winter specific socks, I even bought winter boots but that last measure proved counter productive. My final solution was to buy an electric blanket, put it on the ground and place my feet on top. As I don't really need warming on the upper part of my feet but I don't want them uncovered either I place a towel. This has solved the problem and let me enjoy my coding session confortabily but you can guess this is a drag anytime I need to stand for any reason; go to the bathroom or answer to someone ringing at the main door. By the way, you can tell I'm a remote worker since such setup is not really workable at an office away from home, but I've been working remote from many years now. 

Thinking about my working experience these years, another topic that quickly comes to mind is tiredness. I usually end the working day exhausted. I usually blame the mental load (as if many other professions didn't required focus thinking) but I find it doesn't always relate. Plus, After talking to many other developers it seems to me they don't experience the same condition. People go to the gym and many other activities after work but to me such things become a tall order. From time to time I try, but usually end up failing. I'm getting close to my 50th birthday and this issue remains unsolved. It makes me sad to think maybe I'm just weak or lazy; in the end an individual can only really know his own condition.

Shamefully it has only been this year that I've come to think that these two problems might really be the same. It's not just that remain sitting for many hours makes your returning blood stream more difficult, its that the position presses the femoral vein continuously. This might add up through the hours and affect blood pressure. Staying with an altered blood pressure for a long time might really affect your whole body, as the blood circulation really determines the quality of many bodily functions. This could be the reason why I feel so bad at the end of the day.

But, if that is the case, the problem won't be solved by just warming up my feet; this doesn't makes any sense in summer. Come to think of it my mom has also had circulatory problems all her life, but they showed in a different manner. She had a major condition, had to be medicated and was prescribed special leggings. What she did all her life was to have her legs extended, when watching tv before going to bed. I don't know why I've never thought about doing this while working on a desktop. Maybe I just saw it as unnatural or uncomfortable position.

To lift my legs I'd just need and ajustable stool with no backrest nor any other impediment around the seat. I've end up purchasing a small piano bench and so far, the results have been good. I really think I'm feeling much better after the workday is over. If this carry on for the coming days and extends after winter, this could've become an impactful purchase for me.

For one moment, let's assume this simple purchase works and my circulatory problems improve. What other minor changes could make such an important improvement in my life style? What if I had done this when I was 20? I don't think its an understatement to say that, in a butterfly effect, had my physical condition been better after a working day all this years, my life could've been very different. I could have enjoyed more leisure activities on weekdays, not just weekends. Maybe I'd be more sociable and thus have more friends
