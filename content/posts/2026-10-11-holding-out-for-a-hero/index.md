From childhood, people feel a need to have someone to look up to. For many kids, an athlete, a musician, an actor or, at an early age, even their dad is a hero whose ways they strive to imitate. Naturally, as they mature and learn how flawed people are, they put this need into perspective. Still, it persists, and we see adults showing childlike devotion to unremarkable individuals.

It's as if this need were encoded in our DNA. If that were the case, it would come as little surprise that, in the most successful religion humans have invented, 'a hero' is the cornerstone of the whole creed. I strongly recommend [The Weirdest People](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/51710349), a book by Joseph Henrich that explains the tangible merits of Christian ideas and how they inadvertently shaped the most successful societies in human history. I was educated at a religious institution where much time was spent trying to explain the Holy Trinity. The idea is still a mess to me, so I won't get into specifics: only the second person of the Trinity, 'the Son', Jesus Christ, is relevant to this article. Notice how compelling this figure is: even atheists struggle to badmouth him. Criticism is often directed at religious institutions for taking people's money and property and imposing their morals on the lower classes while granting exceptions to the higher-ups. Jesus, however, is framed as a good-hearted hippy whose teachings were abused by manipulative priests. Indeed, he meets most of the requirements I identify for a suitable hero:

1. Having *skin in the game*
2. Being anti-establishment
3. Having a remarkable life
4. Feeling authentic
5. Having a vaguely described life with many unknowns
6. Being someone you can relate to

I'm curious to see whether, while writing about this list of requirements, I'll be able to find a suitable 'hero' for myself.

**Skin in the game** is an idea popularized by Nassim Taleb:

> To have [skin in the game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_in_the_Game_(book)) is to have incurred risk (monetary or otherwise) by being involved in achieving a goal.
> (...)
> A fund manager that gets a percentage on wins, but no penalty for losing, is incentivized to gamble with his clients' funds.

Returning to Jesus Christ, he faced extreme consequences for trying to kick-start a new religious and ethical system. He was perceived as a threat by the Romans, who believed he was actually claiming political power, and by the Jewish authorities, whose teachings he disapproved of. He paid the ultimate price: torture and death.

![jesus-christ](images/jesus-christ.jpeg)

It's understandable that most people try to minimize risks in their careers; not everybody is cut out to take financial risks in the current environment. But what I really find upsetting is how this mentality translates into ethics and political opinions. Take, for example, these topics: bullfighting, abortion, government spending, immigration, the military, taxes, religion, housing, the stock market, transportation, mining, energy policy or the Middle East. You can predict almost anybody's opinions on any of those topics with a high degree of certainty just by asking which way they lean politically. We don't even have skin in the game when it comes to opinions. That's a testament to how strong the need to belong to a group is. We'd rather accept the whole package of ideas than rock the boat.

Celebrities are never contrarians, although they try to fake it with poor results. Look at the Spanish actors' guild, whose members are huge supporters of the Socialist Party. Their morals coincide with their economic interests, but from time to time, the Socialist Party departs from those morals for practical reasons and, on those occasions, they collectively decide to turn a blind eye. A damning example would be their attitude towards Western Sahara. It was a region administered by Spain from 1884 to 1958, and a Spanish province from 1958 until 1975, when it was conquered by Morocco after the Spanish withdrawal and the defeat of the Sahrawi independence fighters. The Sahrawi cause was a pet cause of the actors' guild for several reasons: anti-colonial sentiment, their antifranquismo (opposition to Francisco Franco, the Spanish dictator), and opposition to an autocratic monarchy (that of Hassan II, king of Morocco). Then, in 2022, Pedro Sánchez, head of the Socialist Party and Spain's prime minister, backed the Moroccan government's plan for Western Sahara and, just like that, the actors' guild's support for the Sahrawi cause vanished.

It takes a huge amount of craziness for a celebrity to be a contrarian. Look at [Sean Strickland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Strickland), the MMA fighter. Describe the guy with any pejorative adjective that comes to mind and you are likely to be right, but now consider his journey from outspoken MAGA and Donald Trump supporter to fierce critic. Strickland's political stance is conservative and America-first, supportive of the working class, and non-interventionist in foreign affairs. Logically, Trump was the obvious choice in 2024, so he supported MAGA during the campaign. Then came the atrocious destruction of the Gaza Strip by the Israelis with US support, and that was where Strickland's disagreements began:

> I cant believe Im saying this but I will vote for a democrat over an aipac republican candidate

Then, in 2026, following the US military strikes against Iran, he accused Trump of putting foreign interests ahead of America's and contradicting the policy of non-intervention he had promised his electorate. He [has listed four areas](https://x.com/SStricklandMMA/status/2098135748432281913?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2098135748432281913%7Ctwgr%5Eaf463cf8e6f281195210e5f11d04cdbbb76cbeda%7Ctwcon%5Es1_c10&ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.sherdog.com%2Fnews%2Fnews%2FSean-Strickland-rips-Justin-Gaethjes-continued-Trump-support-202755) in which he believes Trump has either partly or completely failed to deliver:

- Border security and deportations: Partly fulfilled.
- Avoiding new wars: Failed.
- Cost of living: Failed.
- Government spending: Failed.

To me, this evolution is logical, and his comparison of campaign promises with actual results shows more awareness than many intellectuals display, despite coming from a very imperfect human being.

Although an **anti-establishment** individual has skin in the game, these are different qualities. An entrepreneur always has skin in the game, but is not usually anti-establishment. An Occupy Wall Street activist is anti-establishment, but does not have skin in the game.

![aaron-swartz](images/aaron-swartz.jpg)

[Aaron Swartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Swartz) was a computer programmer and activist who, among other efforts to support the free sharing of information, made a bunch of copyrighted academic journals available to download for free. He was arrested and faced either a sentence of 35 years in prison or a plea deal requiring him to plead guilty. Facing a choice between such a disproportionate penalty and compromising his ethics, he couldn't stand the pressure and committed suicide in 2013.

![Edward Snowden](images/edward-snowden.jpeg)

[Edward Snowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Snowden) is a former American intelligence contractor who exposed a government scheme involving unlawful surveillance of US citizens by their government. He was charged with espionage and fled before facing trial. He was stranded in Russia when the US revoked his passport. He was granted asylum there and still lives there today.

[James Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Watson) was a Nobel Prize winner in physics, one of the discoverers of the structure of DNA, and a racist. In the 19th century, his stance would have been commonplace, but in the 21st, he paid a heavy price for his opinions on the relationship between race, genetics and intelligence. In 2007, he was forced to retire from his position as chancellor of Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory, and in 2019, after reaffirming his beliefs in a documentary, he was stripped of his remaining honorary titles. For him, it seems no topic was off-limits for scientists, unlike most of his peers. It is up to the scientific community to refute his arguments, if discussion of the topic is allowed.

Being **authentic** is a vague personality trait that has to do with being at peace with yourself, with both the good and the bad. Of course, an abusive alcoholic wife-beater might seem authentic, so for this quality to be valuable, it has to be paired with a **remarkable life**. The good has to outweigh the bad, but the latter still has to be visible; otherwise, it backfires and the individual is perceived as inauthentic.

Take, for example, Hollywood actors *Tom Cruise* and *Dwayne 'The Rock' Johnson*. Both became celebrities very early in life, Cruise even earlier than Johnson: he was just 21 when he starred in 'Risky Business'. Both have been methodically building their public personas for years, mastering interviews and showing the bright side that Hollywood wants them to show. However, both are perceived as inauthentic. Their controversies felt like their masks momentarily slipping before they quickly put them back on. In Cruise's case, his Scientology cult and abusive relationships hint at something dark behind all the smiles. Other examples of inauthentic celebrities include *LeBron James*, with his reading habits, and *Lewis Hamilton*, with his support for progressive causes after making his fortune in a [shady](https://youtu.be/H7M74iEonn0?si=i4kmYd5egShFqRoy) and heavily polluting industry, while dangerously driving his opponents into a wall if that was what it took to win.

Granted, criticism is the easy part. Who, then, would be perceived as authentic? To me, *Rafael Nadal* is, in a 'what you see is what you get' way. He is incredibly accomplished, but his limitations are quickly apparent. In fact, those personality traits are part of what made him who he is. You could even say he has embraced his weirdness. *Sean Strickland* qualifies for the reasons stated above.

In reality, the more media exposure you get, the less authentic you seem. That takes me to the next requirement, which is very hard to satisfy nowadays. A hero needs to have a **vaguely described life with many unknowns** in order to 'hide' the misery we all share as humans. How can someone be authentic and hide his flaws at the same time? Well, that's the crux of the matter, and a reason why qualifying as a hero is so difficult. You can manage your online presence and make yourself artificially scarce, but at the same time, what really pays is staying in the spotlight, and the longer you stay out of it, the greater the chance that someone else will replace you. So it's a careful balance that isn't entirely up to you. In his book [The Laws of Human Nature](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/39330937-the-laws-of-human-nature), Robert Greene shows how [Coco Chanel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Chanel) and *Michael Jackson* managed to do exactly that in the pre-internet era. In any case, if the purpose of this blog entry is to pick a hero for myself, I have plenty of pre-2006 personalities to choose from (2006 being my arbitrary cutoff, based on when Facebook opened to the general public).

![coco-chanel](images/coco-chanel.jpg)

A hero needs to be **someone you can relate to**. Compare *Superman* to Jesus Christ: both are almighty, but only one is relatable. This goes for both personality and looks. You can picture yourself having a chat with Jesus; who doesn't like drinking a beer with a hippy, never mind that he can walk on water and come back from the dead? Paradoxically, a comic-book hero feels more out of reach than a god. And, regarding looks, a hero needs to meet a minimum standard; who can relate to someone ugly? Look at how Jesus is portrayed as a handsome man in every picture. These two qualities won't matter if the person is a member of an [out-group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-group_and_out-group). A case in point, for me, is *Pep Guardiola*. He is clearly intelligent and industrious. Just by listening to one of his press conferences, you notice a stark difference between him and his peers. Plus, he was able to learn enough German (a very difficult language) to give instructions in the short time before he joined Bayern Munich as head coach. He is wickedly successful (it's very hard to find a more successful coach), handsome and respectful. Look at how highly he praises Real Madrid, his bitter rival, which must represent everything he hates most: losing (his teams have been eliminated from the Champions League by Real Madrid five times) and Spanish nationalism (Real Madrid proudly displays the Spanish flag and its Spanish identity), given that he is a Catalan nationalist. This puts him in the out-group for me, so despite ticking most of the boxes, he does not qualify as my hero.
